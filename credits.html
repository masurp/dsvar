What is ours, what is borrowed

Credits and licences

This site reuses other people’s data and other people’s charts. Attribution sits beside each one where it is used, and it is collected here too, so there is a single page to check.

THIS SITE

Course material, page design, and every chart built from the code shown on these pages: © Philipp K. Masur, Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, released under CC BY 4.0. Reuse it, change it, teach with it; say where it came from.

That licence covers our work only. Third-party material keeps its own licence, listed below, and some of it is not free to reuse. That includes every data file the site serves: none of them is CC BY 4.0 because the site is, and charts drawn from the three share-alike datasets below carry their licence.

DATA

Every dataset is committed to the repository so the site builds offline. Each is used as published except for subsetting, reshaping and renaming; where more was done, the row says so. The files marked not served are drawn on at render time but not offered for download.

CONDITIONS THAT TRAVEL WITH THE DATA

Three datasets are share-alike, and the files and anything substantially derived from them keep that licence rather than this site’s: the HYG star catalogue and the V-Dem indices (CC BY-SA 4.0), and the CP 1919 profiles (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which is also non-commercial: teaching qualifies, but it is a condition rather than a blanket permission). The van Langren digitisation is non-commercial at its source too.

REPRODUCED CHARTS

Each gallery exhibit that shows an original carries a full credit line directly under the image. Their status divides four ways:

  • Public domain: van Langren (1644), Playfair (1821, Charts No. 1 and No. 3), Minard (1869 and 1858), Nightingale (1858), Snow (1855), Mayr (1877), Sankey (1898), Du Bois and Atlanta University’s chart for the 1900 Paris Exposition (US public domain), and the National Center for Education Statistics’ PISA 2022 table (a US federal work). Credited anyway, because academic practice asks for it even where the law does not.
  • Freely licensed: Hawkins’s warming stripes, Tyler Vigen’s Spurious Correlations chart shown in week 2’s deck, and Rozado, Hughes & Halberstadt’s headline-sentiment figure from PLOS ONE (all CC BY 4.0); Powell’s Hertzsprung–Russell diagram and Drlovedrake’s vote-scaled election cartogram (CC BY-SA); the Reproducibility Project and Many Analysts figures, and Magog the Ogre’s county election map (CC0 1.0); and Gapminder World 2015 (CC BY 4.0; their licence names the wording to use, and the exhibit carries it verbatim: “Based on a free chart from www.gapminder.org”). Each keeps its own licence, not this site’s. Benkler, Faris & Roberts’s media ecosystem map is also openly licensed but more tightly: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which is why it appears here unmodified and with its own caption. Wolfgang Viechtbauer’s forest plot from the metafor documentation is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, shown unmodified on this non-commercial site.
  • Copyrighted, quoted for criticism: Peter Saville’s Unknown Pleasures sleeve, Simon Scarr’s Iraq’s bloody toll, Autodesk Research’s Datasaurus figure, Chris Harrison and Christoph Römhild’s Bible Cross-References, and Orben & Przybylski’s specification curve (Nature Human Behaviour, 2019; shown from the author-deposited accepted manuscript at ORA). Reproduced at low resolution under the Dutch citation right (Auteurswet art. 15a), which permits quotation for criticism and review where the source is named and the extent is proportionate. Every exhibit page is a critique of the specific work it shows, which is what makes this available. These cannot be reused from here.
  • Reproduced by its own author: Figure 3 of Masur & Parry (2026), Technology, Mind, and Behavior. © American Psychological Association; shown here by one of the article’s authors under standard APA author reuse rights for teaching. Not reusable from here.

The full copyright position for every case, including the ones whose original is not shown and why, is in gallery/ORIGINALS.md in the repository.

SOFTWARE

Built with Quarto and R. Charts use ggplot2, with ggalluvial, ggraph, tidygraph, ggrepel, patchwork, ggridges, svglite and ragg. Type is Space Grotesk and IBM Plex Sans, both under the SIL Open Font License.

The design system is forked from the sibling course CADC, retuned to a green palette.

CORRECTIONS

If something here is misattributed, under-credited, or reproduced where it should not be, that is a mistake rather than a position. Say so and it will be fixed or removed. A course about honest charts should be easy to correct.
